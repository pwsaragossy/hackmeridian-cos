# Three lost-cargo cases for the deck: RTX 5090 at Guarulhos 2026, Pearson gold 2023, UAE-India copper 2025

Three cases of cargo that went missing in someone's custody, each followed by a fight over who held it. They are for the deck's opening (owner's order, 2026-10-26). Use them only to show the problem. Never say the rail would have stopped them: dual signing shows who signed, it does not stop theft or a dishonest signer.

1. RTX 5090, Guarulhos (GRU), Oct 2026. [FACT, medium; single-party account] Canaltech, 19 Oct 2026: two pallets of Zotac RTX 5090 imported by the distributor Oderco (Parana) "chegaram ao Aeroporto Internacional de Guarulhos. E lá dentro, sumiram" (Oderco Matos, president). The loss is "R$ 2.500.000" in one passage and "passa de R$ 2.000.000" in another; English press converts it to about US$470,000-495,000 (PC Gamer, Guru3D). The airport had not commented, and no party took responsibility in the article. Everything comes from the distributor's account. Digital Citizen reports that GRU Airport reportedly told the press it registered the loss and started an indemnity process, which Oderco disputes. Police and the Receita Federal had made no statement. Deck wording: "R$2.5M", "vanished inside the airport", "no one has said who held them". Theft is not confirmed, so the deck must not say stolen.
2. Toronto Pearson gold, 17 Apr 2023. [FACT, medium; claims in a lawsuit, untested] CP24/Canadian Press, 11 Oct 2023: about C$20 million in gold (a little over 400 kg) and about US$2 million in cash reached an Air Canada bonded warehouse at about 5:50 p.m. and were released about 40 minutes later to a person with a fraudulent waybill. Brink's sued Air Canada, saying staff accepted the waybill without "verifying its authenticity in any way". Air Canada said it could not comment "as this matter is now before the courts". Peel police charged nine people in April 2024 (Project 24K) and said the suspect presented a fraudulent airway bill, so the deck must not say "no one was held accountable". The civil case ended later: in Jan 2025 the Federal Court capped Air Canada's liability at the Montreal Convention limit, about C$18,500, because the value was not declared; in Aug 2026 the parties settled and the amount was not disclosed ([[pearson-gold-case-ended-court-capped-2002]]). Deck wording: the court capped what the airline owed at about C$18,500.
3. Copper, UAE to India, 2025. [FACT, medium] Khaleej Times, 27 Sep 2025: nearly 49.9 tonnes of copper vanished in transit and the driver left the country. One transport company argued that its responsibility had ended at an earlier stage. The other denied the copper was ever loaded, saying the documents did not prove it. The insurer paid Dh1.67 million. Dubai courts held all defendants jointly liable, and the Court of Appeal upheld that. Deck wording: each company said it was not theirs, and it took a court to decide.
