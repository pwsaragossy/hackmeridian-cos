# YC pitch deck design rules, Kevin Hale 2015: legible, simple, obvious

YC's own deck design rule (Kevin Hale, YC blog, 15 Nov 2015): slides must be legible, simple and obvious. Legible: large, bold type in a simple font, strong contrast with the background, text near the top of the slide. Simple: one idea per slide; split an idea over two slides before crowding one. Obvious: a stranger must say what the slide means at a glance; write the conclusion as the headline or caption instead of making people read a chart. Build the deck from the 5 to 7 ideas people must remember (Demo Day decks have about 5 to 7 slides). Avoid: too much text, caveats, excessive branding on every slide, animations, transitions, memes, captionless photos, maze-like diagrams, screenshots (a short list of steps is better). Applied to a hackathon deck that must cover a fixed list of required topics ([[hackmeridian-2026-rules-and-submission-requirement-5868]]): one required topic per slide, the headline is the sentence a judge can repeat, one supporting line or one number with its source, nothing else.
