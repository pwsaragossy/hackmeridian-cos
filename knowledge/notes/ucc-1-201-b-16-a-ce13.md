# UCC 1-201(b)(16): a document of title must purport to be issued by or addressed to a bailee

The text of UCC section 1-201(b)(16) (opened on the Cornell LII page, 2026-10-25) says that to be a document of title, a document must purport to be issued by or addressed to a bailee, and must also purport to cover goods in the bailee's possession. The page does not say which official text version it shows.

What follows for the Rio-Miami deck and README is an inference, not a legal opinion: a token issued by the exporter or the platform, with no bailee (carrier, warehouse) behind it, does not meet that test by itself, so the safe wording is "tokenized registry linked to the carrier's electronic bill of lading" and never "title to cargo" or "replaces the bill of lading". This matches the copy rule already in PITCH.md ("a token is never title by itself"). The deck needs no legal citation; the cite belongs in the working file only. Radox's counsel confirms the legal reading, because this is US commercial law applied to a Brazil to US shipment and nobody on the team is a lawyer.

Not checked in this note: UCC article 7 (warehouse receipts and bills of lading, including the electronic and control rules), which UCC version a given US state has adopted, Brazilian law on electronic transport documents, and the UNCITRAL MLETR adoption list.
