# DCSA: 45 million bills of lading a year, 1.2 percent electronic in 2021, about 11 percent in 2025, US 6.5 billion direct-cost saving estimate

[FACT, medium; DCSA is a carriers' body that promotes the eBL, an interested party] DCSA, 15 Feb 2023: "Ocean carriers issue around 45 million bills of lading a year." and "In 2021, only 1.2%of these were electronic." The same page says nine member carriers "commit to 100% adoption of an electronic bill of lading (eBL) based on DCSA standards by 2030" and that the move "could save $6.5 billion in direct costs for stakeholders" (a modelled estimate commissioned from McKinsey, report not opened). DCSA newsroom page (undated): "As of 2025, around 11% of bills of lading are issued electronically." The DCSA adoption guide says "5,7% in January 2025" (see [[dcsa-ebl-5-7-percent-jan-6e39]]); the two are not reconciled, so quote one with its date. Secondary: GTR 17 Sep 2024 reports "nearly 5%" in H1 2024. Pages opened 2026-10-25 by a researcher subagent.
