None of them can check what the others say about who held the goods.

Why now: ICC set digital standards for all 36 key trade documents (2024); about US$4B of real-world assets is tokenized on Stellar (SDF, Sep 2026). The custody record is missing.

2
Solution

One shared record. Both companies sign every handover.

handover
Carrier

Buyer
Both sign

The company giving the goods and the one receiving them both sign.

Nobody rewrites

Each step is chained to the one before, so no one can rewrite history.

Anyone checks

A buyer, insurer or lender checks the whole history against one public 32-byte value.

3
Target users

The companies that hand over goods, and the ones that pay on them.

They sign

Warehouses, ports, carriers, customs, buyers

They check

Buyers, insurers, lenders

First corridor: US–Brazil. Later, any Stellar issuer of a tokenized good, through the open spec.

4
Value proposition

Every handover becomes proof that lenders can check.

Lenders see the proof

Lenders and insurers will see which company last signed for the goods before paying.

Companies prove their leg

Your handover carries the receiver's signature, so you can show you passed the goods.

Cargo token, linked record

Each cargo token links to one shipment record of signed handovers.

Proof on Stellar testnet.

Business model

Companies on the route subscribe, and pay per shipment.

Subscription

per connected company

Fee

per shipment on the rail

Open record

checking needs no signature and no account

Every handover has two signers, so each shipment brings in the next company on the route.

Same shape as Swift's published fees: entry, annual, per-message usage (swift.com, accessed 25 Oct 2026). Radox is not affiliated with Swift.
